Robust AIGC Image Detection

A detector for AI-generated images, built around one refusal: don't let an aggregate number stand in for the question a moderation system actually asks. This page is the whole project, start to finish — what each piece is for, what was tried and cut, and why — so anyone touching it, frontend included, can pick it up without re-deriving the reasoning.

The metric you pick decides whether this looks solved

Three findings, read together, are the actual submission — not the model.

Finding 1 — the data 100.00%

accuracy on the standard benchmark corpus (SID_Set) reading zero pixels — real images are 100% JPEG, AI images 100% PNG. A container-format check alone wins the leaderboard.

Finding 2 — the metric 0.35 spread

in true-positive rate across generators at a realistic 1% false-positive budget, hidden entirely inside a AUROC band of 0.97–0.99 that reads as "uniformly strong."

Finding 3 — the fix ~24%, n.s.

Training-time augmentation moves the robustness gap the right direction, but a paired bootstrap says that move isn't statistically significant at 810 real test images — and it doesn't touch the per-generator spread at all.

Brief

The problem & the constraints it had to fit

Hackathon problem statement 5: tell AI-generated images from authentic ones, with accuracy that survives what actually happens to an image before a moderator sees it — re-compression, resizing, blur, noise, cropping, a repost of a repost.

ConstraintValue
Model size< 2B parameters, all branches combined
ComputeHackathon-scale — assume one GPU, Colab T4
Forbidden training dataWildFake reference subset (4,998 COCO val2017 + 8,843 DALL·E Advanced) — disqualification risk if leaked into train
Output contractJSON: {image_path, pred ∈ [0,1]} per image

Every architectural choice traces back to one sentence in the plan: detectors don't fail because AI images are inherently hard to spot — they fail because re-encoding, resizing and blur destroy the low-level artifacts a model was trained to read, and nobody measures that failure because the standard metric doesn't surface it. Measure it first, then try to fix it, then say honestly how much of the problem the fix closed.

Sequence — this order was enforced, not incidental

Build order, and what got cut

The plan's guardrail: "do not reorder the phases — effort-to-payoff ranking is eval harness > augmentation > calibration > fusion gate > backbone choice." Phases 5 and 6 were named as cut, with the reasoning stated, rather than left to look like scope that ran out of time.

1

Robustness eval harness

Done

19-cell degradation grid, validated against a null model (random scores land inside ±3.3σ of chance over 20 seeds) and a planted-weakness model that fails in exactly the injected cell — so a robustness number from it can be trusted before a real model ever runs through it.

2

Leak-audited data pipeline

Done

Found and closed 12 of 13 metadata leak channels in the standard corpus; rebuilt sourcing around three real-photo sources and seven generators, three held fully out of training.

3

CLIP linear-probe baseline

Done

Deliberately the dumbest model that could plausibly work — the control everything else is measured against.

4

Training-time augmentation

Done

The single biggest lever the plan identified. Landed a real, direction-correct improvement on the primary target and did not move the secondary one — reported as exactly that.

5

Artifact branch + fusion gate

Cut

A second model branch reading DCT/high-pass residuals, gated together with the CLIP branch. Cut once Phases 1–4 showed the eval harness and the augmentation ablation mattered more than architecture at this time and compute budget — it wouldn't have earned its own row in the ablation table.

6

Post-hoc calibration

Cut

Per-bucket temperature scaling, so scores would read as real probabilities. Same reasoning as Phase 5 — named as cut, and the consequence (scores are a sigmoid output, not a calibrated probability) is stated plainly in the limitations rather than glossed over.

7

Deliverable surfaces

Done

predict.py and app.py, both running standalone from a fresh clone with the committed checkpoints — no training or data download needed for anyone to see either work.

The part worth explaining out loud

Key decisions, and what they were weighed against

Not every choice below was obvious going in — several are corrections made after something in the data or the numbers contradicted the original plan.

Frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 + a linear head, never fine-tuned

Why

Fits the <2B parameter budget by roughly 13×, and the entire trainable surface is 513 numbers — it trains on a single Colab T4 in minutes, not hours. The plan's guardrail was explicit: don't fine-tune the backbone before the frozen-probe pipeline works end-to-end.

Rejected
A custom CNN or a fine-tuned backbone — more capacity, but it competes with the eval harness and the augmentation ablation for the same time budget, and the plan's own effort-ranking put backbone choice last.

Build the eval harness before any model exists

Why

A robustness number is worthless if the thing measuring it hasn't been checked. The harness had to prove it reports ~0 gap on a random model and correctly flags an injected weakness before it was ever trusted to report a number on a real one.

One canonical decode path for every image, no exceptions

Why

The standard benchmark corpus (SID_Set) separates classes perfectly on container format alone — real images are 100% JPEG, AI images 100% PNG — plus geometry (AI always exactly 1024²) and ICC profiles. A model, or a lazy heuristic, can score 100% having read zero pixels. Every image now goes through: strip all metadata → crop at native resolution (never resize, which leaves a per-class resampling signature) → random, not centered, crop location (generators center their subject) → one JPEG pass at a matched quality (a constant has AUROC 0.5 by construction, provably, not just measured).

Rejected
Using the corpus as distributed, or a lighter cleanup pass — both leave at least one leak channel live, and a leaderboard number built on a leaky corpus measures file-format detection, not generation detection.

Three real-photo sources, not one

Why

AI outputs in the training mix skew polished and cinematic; a single amateur-photo real source lets the model learn "well-lit = fake," a shortcut that survives JPEG re-encoding and would make the robustness numbers look good for the wrong reason. Unsplash (professional photography, labeled real) was added as the control so "polished" stops predicting "fake."

Rejected — and caught by the same audit process
Pexels was tried and dropped after measurement: its files came out ~16 KB against 66–103 KB elsewhere, separable from every other real source by file size alone at AUROC 1.0000. A second-order leak introduced while fixing the first one — now a blocking regression test.

Three entire generators held fully out of training

Why

MidJourney, Gemini (nano-banana), and FLUX.1-dev — three distinct model families, all closed commercial — go to the test split whole, not even a validation slice. That's the realistic deployment case: a generator the model has never seen shows up in the wild. Holding out zero rows of a slice instead of a whole generator would let a hyperparameter choice leak across that boundary.

Report per-generator TPR at a fixed false-positive budget, not just AUROC

Why

AUROC is threshold-free and rank-based — it describes overall separability, not what happens at the one operating point a moderation system actually runs at. Per-generator AUROC sits in a tight 0.97–0.99 band that reads as "uniformly strong." Re-reading the exact same scores at a fixed 1% false-positive budget is what surfaced a 0.35 spread in true-positive rate — a metric change, not a model change, found the real failure mode.

A paired bootstrap for significance, not overlapping confidence intervals

Why

Two runs' marginal 95% CIs overlapping does not mean their difference is non-significant — that comparison throws away the correlation between the two runs' scores on the same images. Resampling the same 6,810 test images once per replicate and applying that resample to both runs gives a CI on the actual difference, which is what let the augmentation result get reported honestly as "not significant at this N" instead of eyeballed as a tie.

Stated, not buried

Limitations

01

810 real test images is the binding constraint, not the model. Every "not significant" result above is a statement about statistical power at this N — not a claim that the effect is zero. More held-out real images is the single highest-leverage next step.

02

FLUX.1-dev is the floor generator, and augmentation didn't move it — same rank, before and after Phase 4. results/error_analysis/ looks at what it's actually missing.

03

No calibration layer. Scores are a trained sigmoid output, not a calibrated probability. acc@0.5 should be read with that in mind; AUROC and TPR@FPR are threshold-free and don't have this problem.

04

No artifact branch, no degradation-awareness at inference time. The augmentation sampler computes a per-image degradation label that nothing currently consumes — kept because it was free, in case a fusion gate comes back in scope.

05

Three held-out generators, not an open set. "Unseen generator" means these three specifically, not a guarantee about every generator not represented in the eval.

For whoever's building the real UI

Where the UI fits — and where it doesn't

app.py is a demo, not the product's frontend

It's a Gradio scratch app whose only job was proving the pitch-video story — upload an image, drag a JPEG-quality slider from 95 down to 30, watch the score move — on the same decode and scoring code the eval grid used, so the demo isn't lying about what the real pipeline does. It was never meant to be styled, extended, or shipped as the product surface, so nothing about disliking its look is a signal to fix app.py — it's a signal to build the real thing against the contract below instead.

That contract is predict.py: an image directory in, preds.json out, one entry per image, byte-identical output across runs and OSes. It recurses jpg/jpeg/png/webp/bmp, converts anything (grayscale, CMYK, alpha) through the same decode path the eval harness uses, and never crashes on a bad file — a genuine decode failure gets pred: null with a warning on stderr instead of stopping the batch.

python predict.py --image_dir path/to/images --out preds.json --ckpt runs/aug.pt [{"image_path": "path/to/images/foo.jpg", "pred": 0.873}, {"image_path": "path/to/images/bar.png", "pred": null}]

Two checkpoints are available: runs/baseline.pt (default) and runs/aug.pt (the augmented one — better on the primary robustness target, per the results above). A real frontend should call this contract, or wrap it behind a small API, rather than embedding anything from app.py.
